What printers know about their own numbers

Keypoint Intelligence's 2024 Color Embellishments survey asked print service providers whether they calculate the cost crossover point between offset and digital.

FigureWhat it meansSource
30% Don't calculate their cost crossover points at all. 1
26% Use outdated methods. Nearly half are quoting without current crossover data. 1
40% Say the barrier is simply not having the data on hand. 1
30% Say the calculation takes too long to be worth doing. 1

Why the number moved

Keypoint Intelligence attributes the shift to two forces moving in opposite directions.

What changedDirectionSource
Offset input costs Expenses tied to makereadies, paper waste, and plates have all risen significantly. 1
Digital cost of ownership Total cost of ownership for large-production digital has fallen by more than half over the past decade, while running far larger monthly volumes than presses from ten years ago. 1
Plate tariffs Proposed duties on aluminum lithographic plates would push offset costs higher still. The Print & Graphic Communications Association, which represents close to 400 print businesses across New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania, has warned the added cost would reach printers and the customers they serve. 2
Run lengths In an early-2026 survey of print buyers, 47% expect their typical run length per job to shrink over the next three years, and 30% have already cut back on orders because of rising costs. 3

There is no single crossover number

Keypoint's group director for production printing demonstrated their estimator on four different shop profiles. Same tool, same market-average data underneath, four very different answers.

Shop profileCrossoverSource
Market averageB2 offset against B2 digital ~10,000 5
High-production offset shop21 shifts a week, 5-minute makeready, 200 sheets waste ~5,000 5
Same shop, but with only a small toner deviceAround 100,000 A3 a month ~800 5
Older offset press, mainly a digital shop30-minute makeready, 600 sheets waste, high-production B2 digital ~18,000 5

A twentyfold spread, and Keypoint expects printers to push back on the average figure and insist theirs is closer to 1,500. The pattern that matters is the direction: the slower your makeready and the heavier your waste, the further up the crossover moves, and the more of your work sits below it.

Two things their estimator leaves out, both of which move the line further toward digital. Finishing isn't counted at all, and Keypoint notes that including it would favor digital further on bound work, where offset needs collation steps digital doesn't. Paper isn't counted either, apart from offset makeready waste.

Keypoint also reports that smaller and mid-size offset shops are increasingly unwilling to fight price wars against larger B1 providers, and are moving toward digital and higher-value work instead. Separately, the concern they hear most often from printers is skilled offset labor: operators retiring, with fewer people training to replace them.
