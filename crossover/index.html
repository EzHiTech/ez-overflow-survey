Do you know your crossover point?

The run length where offset stops being the cheaper way to produce a job has moved a long way in the last decade. Most shops haven't recalculated it.

Here's what the research says, and where it came from.

What printers know about their own numbers

Keypoint Intelligence's 2024 Color Embellishments survey asked print service providers whether they calculate the cost crossover point between offset and digital.

FigureWhat it meansSource
30% Don't calculate their cost crossover points at all. 1
26% Use outdated methods. Nearly half are quoting without current crossover data. 1
40% Say the barrier is simply not having the data on hand. 1
30% Say the calculation takes too long to be worth doing. 1

Why the number moved

What changedDirectionSource
Paper +23% average cost increase since the pandemic. Makeready waste is now more expensive per job. 2
Plates +30% average cost increase over the same period. 2
Digital cost of ownership Total cost of ownership for large-production digital equipment has dropped by more than half over the past decade, while running larger monthly volumes. 1
What that costs on a short run An older press burning 500 makeready sheets to produce a 500-piece job uses two sheets of stock for every good one at today's paper prices. Check it against your last short run. 2

Where the crossover sits today

The run length at which offset becomes the cheaper option, modeled against a current B2 digital press. The better and busier the offset press, the shorter the run it wins.

Your long runs still belong on your press. That math hasn't changed, and nobody is suggesting you replace equipment that's paid for and running well.

The question is the work below your crossover point: the short runs, the versioned jobs, the rush reprints that eat a full makeready to produce a few hundred sheets. That's the work EZ Overflow is built to absorb.

And if that work grows to the point where it belongs on your own floor, we'll tell you. We track the volume with you, and we'd rather be the ones who say when you've outgrown sending it out.
